Function 01 of 05
Planning
"What needs to be done?"
Planning is the first, continuous, and most heavily tested management function. Every other function depends on it.
🔑
Exam Memory Trick
Planning = Predicting
If the question involves deciding, forecasting, setting goals, or creating policies, it belongs to Planning.

What Planning Includes

  • Setting goals, objectives, mission, and vision
  • Forecasting (labor, budget, meals, FTEs)
  • Policies, procedures, and rules
  • Strategic, tactical, and operational planning
  • SWOT analysis
  • Project planning: Gantt, PERT, CPM

Planning Types

  • Strategic: 3 to 5+ years, organization-wide goals
  • Tactical: Department level, bridges strategic to operational
  • Operational: Day-to-day tasks and timelines
  • Contingency: Backup plans when primary strategies fail

High-Yield Exam Examples

Determining FTEs needed next quarter Writing a tray accuracy policy Creating a 5-year strategic plan Scheduling equipment replacement Conducting a SWOT analysis Forecasting meal production

Function 02 of 05
Organizing
"How will we arrange the work?"
Organizing structures people, resources, and authority so the plan can actually happen.
🔑
Exam Memory Trick
Organizing = Structure
If the question involves who reports to whom, workflow design, or arranging resources, it is Organizing.

What Organizing Includes

  • Organizational charts
  • Lines of authority (line, staff, functional)
  • Delegation of tasks and authority
  • Work schedules and workflow design
  • Equipment and space layout
  • Job descriptions and job specifications

Key Distinctions to Know

  • Delegation transfers BOTH responsibility and authority
  • Unity of command: one employee, one supervisor
  • Span of control: number of direct reports one manager supervises
  • Job description: what you do
  • Job specification: what qualifications you need

High-Yield Exam Examples

Creating a production schedule Designing a trayline layout Assigning tasks to employees Clarifying reporting relationships Drawing an organizational chart Delegating ordering authority
✦
👥
Function 03 of 05
Staffing
"Who will do the work?"
Staffing covers the entire employee lifecycle from the first job posting all the way through separation.
🔑
Exam Memory Trick
Staffing = People
If the question involves hiring, training, evaluating, scheduling, or labor law, it is Staffing.

What Staffing Includes

  • Recruitment and hiring
  • Orientation and onboarding
  • Scheduling (master, shift, production)
  • Performance evaluations
  • Competency checks and training
  • Retention strategies
  • Labor laws (FLSA, ADA, EEOC)

Progressive Discipline Order

  • Step 1: Verbal warning
  • Step 2: Written warning
  • Step 3: Suspension
  • Step 4: Termination
FTE = Total paid hours ÷ 40 hrs/week

High-Yield Exam Examples

Interviewing a new cook Completing a performance appraisal Scheduling staff for holidays Ensuring compliance with labor laws Calculating FTEs needed Conducting new employee orientation

Function 04 of 05
Directing
"Let's get the work done."
Directing is the most human and interpersonal of the five functions. It is where plans are actually executed through daily leadership, motivation, and communication.
🔑
Exam Memory Trick
Directing = Daily Leadership
If the question involves motivating, leading, communicating, or resolving conflict, it is Directing.

What Directing Includes

  • Communication (formal and informal)
  • Motivation theories (Maslow, Herzberg)
  • Leadership styles (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire)
  • Conflict resolution
  • Supervision and coaching
  • Soft skills and emotional intelligence
  • Change management

Herzberg: The Big Exam Trap

  • Hygiene factors (pay, conditions, supervision) only PREVENT dissatisfaction
  • Motivators (achievement, recognition, growth) actually DRIVE satisfaction
  • Pay alone does NOT motivate. This is the most tested trap on the exam.
  • Maslow: address the LOWEST unmet need first

High-Yield Exam Examples

Coaching an employee on portioning Mediating a conflict between staff Motivating employees during a busy service Choosing a leadership style for a situation Using active listening with a struggling employee Applying Theory Y management

Function 05 of 05
Controlling
"Did we meet the goal?"
Controlling evaluates performance and ensures standards are met. It loops right back to Planning, making the management cycle continuous.
🔑
Exam Memory Trick
Controlling = Checking
If the question involves monitoring, evaluating, auditing, or correcting, it is Controlling.

The 4 Steps to Controlling

  • Step 1: Establish standards
  • Step 2: Measure performance
  • Step 3: Compare to standards
  • Step 4: Take corrective action

TAFOE: 5 Characteristics

  • Timely: fast enough to act on
  • Accurate: reliable and precise data
  • Flexible: adapts when plans change
  • Objective: free from personal bias
  • Economical: cost must not exceed benefit

High-Yield Exam Examples

Conducting a tray accuracy audit Reviewing budget variance reports Monitoring plate waste Implementing corrective action after low scores Monitoring a critical control point (HACCP) Benchmarking against peer institutions
